# Logical Questions — Answers

These answers are written in my own voice and are based on my memories, preferences and reasoning.

## Q1 — What is your earliest memory? (Mandatory)

One of my earliest memories is going on work trips with my parents when I was around three. They sometimes travelled to remote places. In some places, electricity was often unavailable, and I remember a very large, old silver-coloured flashlight.

While my parents were working, someone from their team would look after me. I also remember that people from other departments seemed to like me.

My father was a deputy head of the group at the time. I know that as background context, though I would not have understood his role when I was that young. The memory itself is only a small fragment, so I do not want to fill in details I cannot clearly remember.

## Q2 — What is the name of your favourite pet, and what other names would you give it?

I like cats because they have a calming, healing energy. The sounds they make help me de-stress.

I would name a cat **Luna**, which means “moon”. It makes me think of a starry sky and a feeling that is cool, gentle and peaceful.

Another name I like is **Lyon**. My constellation is Leo, and a lion is also part of the cat family, so the name has a personal connection for me.

## Q5 — What was the last thing you forgot, and when? (Mandatory)

During university, I worked part-time doing sound at a restaurant where people sang together. One evening, I needed to pick up a mixer from an audio repair shop and bring it to the restaurant for that night's work.

My university was close to the restaurant, but the repair shop was far from both. I was busy with classes and got back late. I went straight to the restaurant and only realised then that I had forgotten to collect the mixer.

I usually arrived about an hour before work started, but by the time I called the repair shop, it had already closed. I called the audio teacher who had trained me and borrowed the teacher's mixer setup for that night.

After that, I started writing down ongoing tasks and setting reminders in different places. I learned not to rely on memory alone when something important needs to be done.

## Q7 — What is the best seat on a bus?

I usually prefer one of the first three seats, as close to the driver as possible. I get motion sickness, and the front feels steadier and less stuffy to me than the middle or the back. I also like having a more open view.

However, my preferred seat is not always the most important thing. I would give priority to older passengers, pregnant women—especially those with a large bump—and passengers with disabilities. I have a personal preference, but I am happy to give up the seat when someone needs it more.

## Q8 — Write a guideline for refuelling a petrol-powered vehicle as quickly as possible.

I would optimise the whole station, not just the time spent filling one vehicle. My goal would be to keep vehicles moving without creating unsafe shortcuts.

1. **Separate the traffic flow.** Have different lanes for two-wheelers and four-wheelers, and separate entry and exit routes to prevent vehicles from blocking each other.
2. **Sort vehicles by fuel type before they queue.** Use clearly marked lanes for the fuel grades available, such as RON 95, RON 97 or E10, so drivers can choose the fuel their vehicle requires before reaching a pump.
3. **Separate payment areas.** Provide distinct areas for cash and online payments. Give cash customers priority because payment can be handed over directly. For online payments, the attendant should start refuelling only after seeing a valid payment ticket or receiving confirmation from the payment terminal.
4. **Assign staff by task.** Have staff handle separate stages, such as guiding vehicles, processing payments and refuelling. This avoids making one employee handle the entire process for every vehicle.
5. **Assign one attendant to each pump.** A pump with two hoses may allow one attendant to serve one or two vehicles, depending on the layout and whether both can be handled safely.
6. **Keep the refuelling step simple and safe.** Once the vehicle is in the correct position and payment is confirmed, the attendant can refuel it while the next vehicles are directed to available lanes.

The main idea is to reduce waiting and bottlenecks through clear traffic flow, early sorting, separate payment handling and parallel work. I would not trade basic safety for speed.
